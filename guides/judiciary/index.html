How selection works, stage by stage

Each stage below is a real gate. Only the last one leads to an appointment letter.

  1. 1

    Preliminary Exam

    100-150 Qs — Law (Civil/Criminal/Constitutional), GK, English. Objective type.

    Qualifying. Cut-off varies by state and category.

  2. 2

    Main Exam (Written)

    3-4 papers: Civil Law, Criminal Law, English, Language (Hindi/Regional). Descriptive.

    Merit prepared from Main score. 3 hrs per paper.

  3. 3

    Interview

    20-30 min panel. Legal knowledge, personality, aptitude for judicial service.

    200 marks. Final merit = Main + Interview.

  4. 4

    Training & Posting

    6-12 months judicial training at state judicial academy.

    Posted as Civil Judge / Judicial Magistrate after training.

How to apply, in five steps

Almost every central and state portal follows this order. Complete each step before moving on — most portals stop letting you edit once the fee is paid.

  1. 1

    Read the official notice

    Confirm qualification, age as on the notice's cut-off date, fee and the last date before you register.

  2. 2

    Register on the official portal

    Use the link on the notice, not a search result. Keep the registration number and password it gives you.

  3. 3

    Fill the form, upload photo and signature

    Enter your name exactly as on your Class 10 certificate. Upload the photo and signature in the size and format the portal specifies.

  4. 4

    Pay the fee

    Pay online and save the receipt. If you claim a fee waiver, the category certificate must already be in the prescribed format.

  5. 5

    Download the confirmation

    Save the submitted application PDF and the payment receipt. Check the portal again after a day to confirm the form was accepted.

Documents to keep ready

Scan these before the form opens. A missing certificate is the most common reason an otherwise eligible application is rejected at verification.

  • Passport photograph

    Recent, plain light background, within the portal's file-size limit.

  • Signature

    Signed in black ink on white paper, scanned at the size the portal asks for.

  • Class 10 certificate

    Proves your date of birth and is the usual minimum qualification.

  • Degree or marksheet

    Final or provisional, whichever the notice says it accepts.

  • Caste or EWS certificate

    In the prescribed format from the competent authority, valid on the cut-off date.

  • Domicile or residence proof

    Needed only where the notice reserves posts for a state or region.

  • Photo ID

    Aadhaar, PAN, passport or voter card — the name must match the application.

  • Fee payment proof

    UPI or bank receipt, kept until the admit card is issued.

Exam syllabus & pattern

Official syllabus and exam pattern for this sector. Always verify on the recruiting authority's website.

Prelims — Civil Judge (Junior Division)

Objective paper on substantive and procedural law plus general knowledge, with negative marking in most states. Marks, sections and the number of questions are fixed by each High Court's notification.

  • Civil law — Code of Civil Procedure, Indian Contract Act, Transfer of Property Act, Specific Relief Act, Limitation Act, Hindu Marriage Act, Indian Succession Act
  • Criminal law — Indian Penal Code / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Code of Criminal Procedure / Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, Indian Evidence Act / Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam
  • Constitutional and other law — Constitution of India, torts, consumer protection, family law, local rent and land laws of the state

Mains — written papers

Written papers in civil law, criminal law, English (essay and precis) and the state language or Hindi, with a translation paper. Several states add judgment writing. Each High Court sets its own paper structure and marks.

  • Law papers — Problem-based questions that require applying sections and citing case law, not reciting definitions
  • Language papers — Essay, precis, comprehension and translation — qualifying in some states but counted in others
  • Judgment writing — Framing issues, marshalling facts, applying law and writing a reasoned order

Interview / viva voce

Oral examination on law, procedure and the candidate's background, conducted by the High Court's selection committee after the mains.

  • What is asked — Leading cases, procedural steps, recent amendments, legal current affairs, and the candidate's practice or academic specialisation

Bare Acts and case law — the real syllabus

Judicial service exams are set from bare Acts and leading judgments. State notifications list the exact statutes, and the list differs between states.

  • How to read — Read the bare Act section by section and annotate it with leading cases; do not rely on guides alone, because the papers quote sections directly
  • Case law — Constitutional law and procedural law judgments of the Supreme Court; recent amendments to criminal law

When and how to prepare

  1. 1
    Months 1–6 — Bare Acts

    Read CPC, CrPC/BNSS, IPC/BNS, Evidence Act/BSA and the Constitution from the bare Act, section by section, annotating leading cases. Guides summarise; the papers quote sections.

  2. 2
    Months 4–9 — State-specific law

    Add the state rent control, land revenue and local laws named in your High Court's notification. This is where candidates who studied only central law lose marks.

  3. 3
    Months 6–12 — Writing practice

    Judicial mains is problem-based. Write answers applying the section to the facts, then cite one or two cases. Practise judgment writing and the essay paper weekly.

  4. 4
    Month 9 onward — Previous papers

    Solve previous papers of your state and two neighbouring states. Prelims repeats sections and case names; track every wrong answer by statute.

  5. 5
    Final month — Revision and viva

    Revise annotated bare Acts and your case list. Prepare for the viva: recent Supreme Court judgments, amendments and your own academic or practice background.

Career path after joining

Promotions run on seniority plus departmental exams. The rung you enter on depends on the post you clear.

  1. 1 Junior Court Assistant / Clerk (Level 2-4)
  2. 2 Civil Judge / Judicial Magistrate (Level 10)
  3. 3 Senior Civil Judge (Level 11)
  4. 4 District & Sessions Judge (Level 12)
  5. 5 High Court Judge (Level 16-17)

What the notification does not tell you

The parts candidates usually discover too late.

  • Judicial service exams are state-specific. Each state has its own syllabus and pattern.

  • PCS(J) is one of the most competitive exams. 10,000+ applicants for 100-200 vacancies.

  • Law degree (LLB) is mandatory. 3-year or 5-year LLB both accepted.

  • Some states require 2-3 years of practice for HJS (Higher Judicial Service).

  • Judges have job security but heavy workload. 50-100 cases per day in trial courts.

  • Salary: Civil Judge ~₹77,840 (Level 10) + DA + HRA. District Judge ~₹1,44,840 (Level 12).

Frequently asked questions

What is PCS(J) and how is it different from UPSC?

PCS(J) = Provincial Civil Service (Judicial). State-level exam for Civil Judge posts. UPSC conducts exams for central services (IAS, IPS). Different eligibility, syllabus, and recruiting body.

Can a 3-year LLB graduate apply for judicial service?

Yes. 3-year LLB after graduation or 5-year integrated LLB both accepted. Some states require minimum age 21-22 and maximum 35.

Is practice experience required for judicial service?

For lower judiciary (Civil Judge): no experience required in most states. For higher judiciary (District Judge): 7 years practice required. Check state-specific rules.

What is the age limit for judicial service exams?

Civil Judge: 21-35 years (varies by state). District Judge: 35-45 years. Age relaxations for SC/ST/OBC/PwD.

How many vacancies are there in judiciary?

UP: 100-200 (Civil Judge) annually. Bihar: 100-150. Total judicial vacancies exceed 2000 per year across India. High Courts recruit separately.

Where do I find official judiciary notifications?

State PSC websites (uppsc.up.nic.in, bpsc.bihar.gov.in) and High Court websites. SarkariJobs+ links the official notification on every listing.
